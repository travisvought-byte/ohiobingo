Where the rules come from

Charitable bingo in Ohio is governed by state law and licensed by the Ohio Attorney General's Charitable Law Section. We share what we hear from operators and players with legislators and regulators, and we report back here.

Veteran Home Guardians is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. It does not support or oppose candidates for office.

Bills to watch

These bills were introduced in the 136th General Assembly (2025 to 2026) and would change Ohio's charitable gaming law. Bills move and stall, so open the bill page for where each one stands today.

  • House Bill 344 Would let 501(c)(3) charities offer electronic instant bingo at bars and restaurants, authorize video lottery terminals at retailers, tax electronic instant bingo, and widen the hours veterans and fraternal organizations may offer instant games.
  • House Bill 476 Would authorize online raffles under the Charitable Gaming Law, on platforms approved by the Attorney General.
  • Senate Bill 197 A broad gambling bill. Among much else, it would move regulation of charitable bingo from the Attorney General to the Ohio Casino Control Commission on January 1, 2027.
  • Senate Bill 399 Would modify the Attorney General's authority under the Charitable Gaming Law.

Summaries are drawn from the bill titles and the Legislative Service Commission's analyses. A bill that has not passed by the end of 2026 must be reintroduced in the next General Assembly.

Rules operators ask about

These are current law. We have not found a bill this session that changes them. If one of them shapes how your game runs, your legislators should hear it from you.

  • Volunteers only. A charity may not pay the people who work its bingo game, and they may not accept pay from any source. Some operators say this makes it hard to staff a game as volunteers age. ORC 2915.09
  • No paid outside help. A charity running traditional bingo may not pay consulting fees for services related to its session. ORC 2915.09
  • Prize and session caps. No more than $6,000 in traditional bingo prizes per session, and no more than three sessions in seven days for most organizations. ORC 2915.09
  • Checks and bank transfers only. Bingo expenses and distributions must be paid by check or electronic fund transfer from the bingo account. ORC 2915.10
  • The Attorney General's rules. The administrative rules for charitable bingo list a five-year review date of December 23, 2026. OAC Chapter 109:1-4
  • Electronic instant bingo rules. The Attorney General's rules for electronic instant bingo systems are also due for review by December 23, 2026, and veterans and fraternal groups report that changes are being drafted. Proposed rules, hearing dates and how to comment are posted on the Register of Ohio. OAC Chapter 109:1-7

What to say

  1. Say who you are. Your name, your town, and the hall or cause you are part of.
  2. Name the bill or the rule. One subject per message. Use the bill number or the code section.
  3. Tell them what it does to your game. Players lost, volunteers stretched, dollars that did or did not reach your cause. Your own numbers carry more weight than anyone's talking points.
  4. Ask for one thing, and thank them for their time.

Speak for yourself and your organization. We do not send messages on anyone's behalf.
